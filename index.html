01 · A life

Her name was Jina.

Jina (Mahsa) Amini was a 22-year-old Kurdish woman from Saqqez. In September 2022, she travelled to Tehran with her brother.

On 13 September she was detained by Iran’s “morality police” over the country’s compulsory veiling rules. She fell into a coma and died in custody in hospital on 16 September.

JINA

Jina means life.

Her Kurdish name became inseparable from a movement that insists life must be lived with dignity, equality, and choice.

02 · A cry becomes a movement

Jin. Jiyan. Azadî.

At Jina’s funeral in Saqqez on 17 September, Kurdish women raised the chant “Jin, Jiyan, Azadî” — Woman, Life, Freedom. Rooted in the Kurdish women’s movement, it travelled across Iran in Persian and across the world in many languages.

01

Woman

Bodily autonomy. Equal presence in public life. The right to speak, choose, create, and lead.

02

Life

A life beyond fear: ordinary, joyful, safe, and fully one’s own.

03

Freedom

The freedom to think, assemble, love, dissent, and imagine a different society together.

The movement placed women and girls at the centre while connecting demands for gender equality, dignity, justice, and freedom across communities and generations.

03 · A movement in time

From one name to millions of voices.

This is not a complete history. It is a set of turning points that help us understand why remembrance remains active work.

Before 2022

The words already carried history.

“Jin, Jiyan, Azadî” emerged from decades of Kurdish women’s organizing and thought.

13 September 2022

Jina is detained in Tehran.

The “morality police” detained her over compulsory veiling rules.

16 September 2022

Jina dies in custody.

Her death becomes the spark for nationwide protest.

17 September 2022

The chant rises in Saqqez.

At her funeral, “Jin, Jiyan, Azadî” is chanted and shared far beyond the cemetery.

Autumn 2022

The uprising spreads.

Women and young people lead demonstrations across Iran; communities worldwide gather in solidarity.

24 November 2022

An international investigation begins.

The UN Human Rights Council establishes an independent fact-finding mission on violations connected to the protests.

March 2024

The UN mission publishes its findings.

It finds the State responsible for Jina’s unlawful death following physical violence and concludes that serious violations included crimes against humanity.

15 September 2026

We gather in Vienna.

Four years on, remembrance becomes encounter: groups, artists, activists, and neighbours meet in one shared civic space.
